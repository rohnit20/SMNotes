🎯 What Is Strategy?
Module 1Foundational

Strategy is a fundamental pattern of objectives, resource deployments, and interactions with the environment. From Greek Strategia — the art of the military commander.

Planning vs. Strategy
💡 Exam tip: Planning says "We will open 10 stores in Year 1." Strategy says "We will out-compete rivals by becoming the co-working hub for Gen Z."

Each nation had different talent levels AND different strategies. Outcomes differed not because one army was smarter, but because strategies were better aligned with objectives and resources — your professor's point: strategy IS the multiplier on talent.

📐 Mintzberg's 5 Ps of Strategy
Module 1Likely Exam Q

Henry Mintzberg argued that "strategy" is not one concept — it has 5 distinct meanings. Real strategy is always a blend of deliberate (planned) and emergent (unplanned) patterns.

The 5 Ps — Visual Diagram
PNameDefinitionExample
1PlanDeliberate, intended course of action made in advanceApple's 3-year product roadmap
2PloyTactical maneuver to outsmart a rivalSamsung announcing a product to deter Apple's launch
3PatternConsistent behavior over time — emergent strategyApple consistently making premium products (even if never formally planned)
4PositionFinding the right market nicheApple positioning as "premium, design-first" tech
5PerspectiveFirm's worldview, culture, and valuesApple's culture: "Think Different"
⭐ EXAM MODEL ANSWER: Explain Mintzberg's 5 Ps

Mintzberg argued strategy cannot be defined by a single concept — it has 5 Ps: Plan (deliberate, intended action), Ploy (tactical maneuver to outsmart rivals), Pattern (consistent behaviour = emergent strategy), Position (locating the firm in the right market niche), and Perspective (the shared organisational worldview and culture driving all decisions). Real-world strategy is always a blend of deliberate plans and emergent patterns — what a firm intends vs. what actually happens.

⭐ EXAM TIP: Common Confusion

Students often mix up Vision and Mission. Vision = where you want to BE in the future. Mission = why you exist TODAY. Strategic Intent is the most extreme/emotional level — it's almost like an obsession (Komatsu: "Encircle Caterpillar").

⭐ EXAM: Distinguish SBU Strategy vs Functional Strategy

SBU: Scope = one business unit; Focus = how to compete in the market; Who = SBU top execs; Time = medium-long. Functional: Scope = one department (HR, Mktg, etc.); Focus = how to support SBU/corporate goals; Who = dept. heads; Time = short-long. SBU asks "who to beat and how?" Functional asks "how does my department help us beat them?"

✈️ SpiceJet — Turnaround Case Study
Module 1Case Study

SpiceJet is a real-world application of Retrenchment → Turnaround strategy combined with Cost Leadership at the business level.

⭐ EXAM MODEL ANSWER: SpiceJet

SpiceJet represents Retrenchment → Turnaround in corporate strategy. Facing collapse in 2014 (cash crunch, DGCA warnings, mass cancellations), Ajay Singh executed a textbook turnaround: leadership change, cost restructuring (lease renegotiation, fleet optimization to fuel-efficient 737 Max), revenue diversification (SpiceXpress cargo), and route rationalization. Business-level strategy remained Cost Leadership (LCC model). The strategic lesson: turnaround requires BOTH operational restructuring AND strategic repositioning simultaneously.

⚡ Porter's Five Forces + The 6th Force
Module 2Core Tool — Apply to ANY Industry Case

Michael Porter (1979). Purpose: Assess the attractiveness and profitability potential of an INDUSTRY. Note: This is an industry-level tool, not firm-level.

The Five Forces Diagram
ForceHIGH (Unattractive) when…LOW (Attractive) when…
New EntrantsLow capital, no patents, easy entryHigh capital, patents, brand loyalty, regulations
Supplier PowerFew suppliers, critical inputs, can forward integrateMany suppliers, commoditised inputs, easily switched
Buyer PowerFew large buyers, volume purchases, standardised productsMany small buyers, highly differentiated products
SubstitutesEasy to substitute, low switching costHigh switching cost, no functional alternatives
RivalryMany equal competitors, slow growth, low differentiationFew competitors, rapid growth, high differentiation
ComplementorsNo complementors = product less valuableStrong complementors = ecosystem adds value to your product
📱 Case: Apply to Smartphone Industry

New Entrants: LOW threat — high R&D, manufacturing, brand investment needed

Supplier Power: MEDIUM — TSMC/Samsung fab chips, Apple/Android OSes have some power

Buyer Power: MEDIUM — consumers can switch brands easily

Substitutes: LOW — no real substitute for smartphones in 2024

Rivalry: HIGH — Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo compete intensely

Complementors: STRONG — App developers, accessory makers, cloud services all add value

🔗 Value Chain Analysis
Module 2Michael Porter, 1985 — Competitive Advantage
💡 MARGIN = Total Value Created − Total Cost of all activities. Find where you can reduce cost (Cost Advantage) OR add uniqueness (Differentiation Advantage).
🛒 Case: Walmart's VCA (Cost Leadership Example)

Inbound Logistics: Cross-docking warehouses, direct supplier relationships, just-in-time delivery → massive cost savings

Operations: Standardised store layouts, RFID inventory tracking → efficient

Technology: Proprietary Retail Link system shares real-time sales data with suppliers → reduces waste

Result: Every part of the value chain optimised for LOWEST COST → passes savings to customers → competitive advantage

🌪️ VUCA World
Module 2Key — connects to RBV exam question

Coined by the US Military in the late 1990s to describe the post-Cold War environment. Now the dominant lens for understanding modern business environments.

⭐ PROFESSOR'S EXAM QUESTION: How is RBV related to VUCA?

Point 1: VUCA makes RBV MORE relevant — in stable environments, Porter's external positioning suffices. But in VUCA, entire industries are disrupted overnight (Netflix vs. Blockbuster). External positions become fragile. Internal resources (RBV) are more durable and portable across contexts.

Point 2: VUCA amplifies VRIO value — under Volatility, rare resources extend time advantage. Under Ambiguity, firms with deep TACIT knowledge (intangible RBV resources) can "read" unclear signals faster than rivals.

Point 3: Dynamic Capabilities = RBV adapted for VUCA — continuously SENSE change, SEIZE opportunities, RECONFIGURE internal resources. This is RBV in action within VUCA context.

Conclusion: VUCA describes the context; RBV/VRIO prescribes the internal response. Both are complementary — not competing theories.

💎 Resource-Based View (RBV) & VRIO
Module 2Professor's Exam Question
VRIO Flowchart
Two Foundational Properties of RBV Resources
PropertyMeaningWhy It Matters
HETEROGENEOUSDifferent firms possess different resource bundlesNot all firms have the same quality of resources — some genuinely have better ones
IMMOBILEResources don't freely flow between firmsYou can't just buy what makes a rival special — resources are "sticky"
💡 If resources were homogeneous AND mobile, any firm could copy any other instantly → no sustained advantage possible. These two properties explain WHY competitive advantage can last.
What Makes a Resource Costly to Imitate? (3 Reasons)
  • Unique Historical Conditions — firm was in the right place at the right time (path dependency — cannot be replicated)
  • Causal Ambiguity — even if rivals CAN SEE the resource, they can't figure out WHY it works
  • Social Complexity — involves trust, culture, relationships — impossible to simply copy by hiring individuals
🏄 Red Ocean vs. Blue Ocean Strategy
Module 2Kim & Mauborgne, 2005
The ERRC Framework (Four Actions)
🎪 Case: Cirque du Soleil — The Classic Blue Ocean

ELIMINATE: Animals (expensive, controversial) · Multiple show arenas · Aisle concession sales

REDUCE: Star performer cost · Danger/thrill (replaced by artistry)

RAISE: Unique venue/tent experience · Production quality

⭐ CRITICAL: BCG vs. GE vs. Ansoff — Different categories!

BCG and GE = Portfolio Analysis tools → "Which of our existing SBUs should we invest in?"

These answer COMPLETELY DIFFERENT questions — do NOT confuse them in a case study!

🏆 Porter's Generic Competitive Strategies
Module 3BrewBean Case — High Exam Probability
⭐ "Stuck in the Middle" — Critical Concept

A firm trying to pursue ALL three simultaneously ends up with: no clear competitive advantage, above-average costs, below-par differentiation, no niche expertise → below-average industry performance. Porter calls this "stuck in the middle."

☕ BrewBean Café — Apply Porter's Generic Strategies

Operations Head: Simplify menu, automate, bulk buy → lower prices = Cost Leadership

Marketing Head: Exclusive blends, personalised experience, premium prices = Differentiation

Business Dev Head: Focus only on university campuses = Focus Strategy

CEO's concern: Pursuing all three = Stuck in the Middle!

Recommendation: Given BrewBean's 5-year premium heritage → Differentiation Focus — double down on the premium co-working café experience for urban millennials/Gen-Z.
